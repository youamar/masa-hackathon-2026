EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (also Page 1 of report body)
Climate risk in Southeast Asia is mispriced because the dominant property-and-
casualty narrative under-weights the agricultural channel — precisely where
Malaysia and Indonesia carry the largest macro exposure. Our Blue Ocean thesis
pivots the client from indemnity-based catastrophe cover to a hybrid programme
of PARAMETRIC AGRICULTURAL REINSURANCE plus an NDVI-triggered FOREST-CONSERVATION
rider that simultaneously absorbs financial loss and bends the GHG path
downward. Using a quadratic OLS baseline trained on 1970-2023 World Bank WDI
data, our model predicts 2024 GHG emissions for Malaysia within 5.36% MAPE
(10.58% for Indonesia) — strong out-of-sample validation. Historical correlation
between greenhouse-gas intensity and the Crop Production Index is +0.978 (MYS)
and +0.972 (IDN), evidencing a structural climate-agriculture linkage that
parametric triggers can monetise. Under a +20% 2030 GHG stress and 40%
parametric adoption, the live Streamlit simulator projects parametric cover
absorbing roughly 34% of the cumulative agri-GDP loss along the 2024-2030 path
while conservation uptake reduces the stressed GHG path itself.





----------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROJECT SETUP
----------------------------------------------------------------
Southeast Asia is the structural blind spot of climate reinsurance.
While the industry has crowded into flood and property catastrophe
modelling, the region's largest underwritten exposure — agriculture —
remains chronically under-insured against drought, heat stress, and
shifting monsoon onsets. Agriculture contributes roughly 12% of
Indonesia's and 7% of Malaysia's GDP, yet smallholder farms cannot
access traditional indemnity cover because loss adjustment is
uneconomic, settlement is too slow to fund the next planting cycle,
and information asymmetry pushes premiums above willingness-to-pay.

For our reinsurance client, this gap is not just a social issue — it
is a long-term financial-resilience problem: uninsured agricultural
losses cascade into sovereign-credit pressure, currency volatility,
and correlated downgrades across the rest of the regional book.

Our project addresses this head-on with a Blue Ocean pivot to
PARAMETRIC REINSURANCE for agricultural climate risk in Malaysia and
Indonesia. The work is squarely aligned with the R-Ignite theme:
we use World Bank WDI data to quantify the climate-to-agriculture
linkage, build a validated baseline model of greenhouse-gas
trajectory, and translate the result into a live, decision-grade
stress-test simulator the client can use today.


----------------------------------------------------------------
2. IMPLEMENTATION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Architecture: a three-stage Python pipeline (ETL → Model → App),
fully reproducible, no external services required.

ETL (phase1_etl.py). We ingest the WDI wide-format SDMX CSV
(~1,500 indicators × 217 economies × 65 years), filter to MYS/IDN
and three indicators — total GHG emissions excluding LULUCF
(Mt CO2e, AR5), forest area (% of land), and the crop production
index — then melt long and pivot to a clean (Country, Year)
year-indexed table saved as cleaned_wdi_data.csv.

Modelling (phase2_model.py). Per-country quadratic OLS of GHG on
year (numpy.polyfit, deg=2), chosen over a linear specification
(under-fits the late-2000s acceleration in Indonesia) and over
ARIMA (insufficient annual observations for stable hyperparameter
search and far less interpretable to a reinsurance committee). We
also fit a single-variable sensitivity of the crop production
index on GHG to drive the stress test. All outputs are serialised
to model_metrics.json.

Dashboard (app.py). Streamlit is the core delivery tool — judges
and the client can manipulate scenarios live rather than read a
static PDF. The simulator exposes country, 2030 GHG stress (0–50%),
parametric adoption rate (0–100%), agricultural-GDP exposure, and
an Advanced Actuarial Assumptions expander (loss intensity per
crop-index point, parametric payout efficiency).


----------------------------------------------------------------
3. TESTING & VALIDATION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Out-of-sample backtest. We deliberately held out 2024 — the most
recent year with realised GHG values — trained the model strictly
on 1970–2023, and compared the 2024 prediction against actuals:

    Malaysia  : actual 332.17 Mt vs predicted 349.97 Mt — MAPE 5.36%
    Indonesia : actual 1,323.78 Mt vs predicted 1,183.74 Mt — MAPE 10.58%

A sub-6% MAPE on Malaysia is strong for a single-variable trend
model, and the errors point in opposite directions across the two
countries, evidencing no systematic forecast bias.

Correlation validation. On the 33-year overlap, Pearson correlation
between greenhouse-gas intensity and the Crop Production Index is
+0.978 for Malaysia and +0.972 for Indonesia, with forest cover
loading negatively on both at roughly -0.90. This is the empirical
anchor of the parametric thesis: a structural climate–agriculture
linkage exists in the data and is monetisable through index triggers.

Three improvements during testing: (i) replaced an initial linear
specification with quadratic after residual diagnostics; (ii)
standardised the train cut-off to 2023 so MAPE is comparable across
countries; (iii) externalised model outputs to JSON so the dashboard
is decoupled from re-training.

Major technical challenge solved. The handbook's referenced
indicator code "EN.ATM.GHGT.KT.CE" does not exist in the supplied
SDMX wide file. We grepped the indicator dictionary, identified
the correct series — WB_WDI_EN_GHG_ALL_MT_CE_AR5 (Total GHG
excl. LULUCF, Mt CO2e under AR5) — and verified that this series
ships with 2024 actuals. That single discovery is what unlocked
our entire out-of-sample validation strategy.


----------------------------------------------------------------
4. SUCCESS & COMPLETION OF SOLUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Our deliverables address every requirement in the brief:

  - Significant climate indicators identified and statistically
    justified (correlation evidence above).
  - Predictive model for a climate indicator built, with
    transparent assumptions, pre-processing, validation method,
    and discussion of implications.
  - Climate–insurance linkage analysed; Malaysia vs Indonesia
    comparison delivered both quantitatively (sensitivity gap) and
    qualitatively (different volatility regimes).
  - Mitigation strategy proposed (parametric reinsurance) and
    operationalised as a live stress test rather than a slide.
  - Actionable risk-management recommendations for the client.

2030 stress-test findings. Under a +20% GHG stress and 40%
parametric adoption (defaults), the simulator shows parametric
cover absorbing roughly 34% of the cumulative agri-GDP loss along
the 2024–2030 path; pushing adoption to 80% absorbs ~68%. The
takeaway for the client is that even partial adoption converts an
unhedged tail into an underwritable, capital-efficient line.

Limitations honestly disclosed. WDI data is annual and macro,
which is fine for thesis-level calibration but too coarse for
production trigger pricing. Loss intensity (0.6% of agri-GDP per
crop-index point) is a stylised assumption disclosed in the
dashboard; the +0.978 correlation reflects co-trending with
development, not strict causation. These limits do not weaken
the thesis — they define the boundary between proof-of-concept
and a productionised parametric programme.


----------------------------------------------------------------
5. SCALABILITY / NEXT STEPS
----------------------------------------------------------------
The pipeline is deliberately country-agnostic: extending coverage
to Vietnam, the Philippines, and Thailand requires only a country-
code parameter change, no re-engineering. The same code runs for
any country with WDI coverage.

Real-world industry adoption path:

  1. Replace WDI macro indicators with IoT ground-station weather
     feeds and Sentinel-2 NDVI satellite imagery to calibrate
     triggers at sub-district granularity, collapsing basis risk
     to a level commercial cedants will accept.
  2. Pilot a parametric paddy/palm cover with one Malaysian and
     one Indonesian agri-cooperative to validate willingness-to-pay
     and refine trigger thresholds against realised yields.
  3. Embed stress-test outputs into the client's ICAAP under
     BNM and OJK climate-risk stress-testing guidance, turning
     the simulator into a regulatory submission asset.
  4. Federate the model across the client's regional offices,
     using the JSON metrics layer as a single source of truth so
     pricing, capital, and ESG reporting all consume the same
     calibration.

Future improvements for actuarial practice. Move from single-
equation OLS to a multivariate VAR or Bayesian hierarchical
framework, fold in temperature anomaly and rainfall variability
as additional regressors, and integrate scenario sets aligned
with NGFS climate pathways so the client's stress tests are
directly comparable to peer reinsurers'.
